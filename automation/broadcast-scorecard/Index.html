How the numbers work

  • Cost / click = AiSensy cost ÷ clicks. Spent above average = what a broadcast cost beyond its clicks × the campaign’s average cost per click: money that bought clicks at an above-average price.
  • Slot verdicts: Keep: top slot (15%+ more clicks than similar, at or below average cost per click). Keep. Keep slot, use the better message (one week’s message clearly beat the others). Improve the message. Cut this slot (40%+ below similar with solid evidence, or cost per click over 2.5× average). “If cut” shows the spend saved and clicks lost per webinar.
  • Live window: reminders between 10:50–11:40 AM and 7:00–8:05 PM. A reminder is suggested for dropping when its cost per click is 40%+ above the live-window average and it does worse than similar sends.
  • Click rate = clicks ÷ delivered. Read rate = reads ÷ delivered. Click-to-read = clicks ÷ reads, which shows how persuasive the message is once opened. Cost / click = AiSensy cost ÷ clicks.
  • vs similar compares a broadcast’s clicks with what similar broadcasts got: same campaign, same timing (1+ days before, webinar morning, 1–6 h before, final hour, during live, next day) and same list size (registered list vs 5,000+). +40% means 40% more clicks than expected.
  • Verdicts are per message (all sends of nearly the same text). Use more: 25%+ above similar with solid evidence. Keep sending: within 10% of similar or better. Improve: below similar, with the likely reason (opened less, or read but not clicked). Remove: 40%+ below similar with solid evidence, or no clicks where some were expected. Not enough data: under 300 delivered or too few expected clicks.
  • Confidence reflects how far the result is from chance, based on the number of clicks expected. High = the gap is very unlikely to be luck.
  • Campaigns are recognised from the message text. Webinar sessions are found from the bursts of live-time sends, so a webinar moved to another day (e.g. the Tuesday webinar held on Monday 19 Oct) is picked up automatically and uses its usual schedule. Slots that fall on a day when another webinar ran are marked as skipped, not missed. Each broadcast is matched to the nearest slot in the Tuesday, Wednesday or Sunday schedule (within 15 minutes around live time, 90 minutes otherwise).
